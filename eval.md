# No AI slop eval

Use this after the rewrite. Answer each check with pass or fail. If any check fails, fix the draft before returning it.

For detect requests, make sure the response names each pattern found with a quoted line and a short fix, without rewriting the draft.

## Editing principles

1. Does the edit preserve the user's point without adding claims, examples, stats, quotes, or opinions?
2. Does it preserve the writer's distinctive vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, uncertainty, digressions, and level of polish?
3. Does it leave strong human sentences alone instead of rewriting them for consistency or making every paragraph equally tidy?
4. Is the amount of cutting proportional to the actual slop, with no aggressive compression that strips out character?
5. Does the draft lead with what the reader needs while keeping personal setup that adds context, tension, or character?
6. Are points front-loaded where that improves clarity without forcing every unit into the same structure?
7. Do sentences earn their place, with concrete facts, protected details, and direct verbs where the draft supports them?
8. Does the draft use active voice with human subjects where possible?
9. Does the edit keep useful edge and preserve structure unless the structure was hurting the piece?
10. Are genuinely tangled sentences fixed while clear spoken cadence, fragments, and changes in pace remain intact?
11. Did the edit avoid faking imperfection — no added typos, manufactured slang, forced "umms/ahhs," invented anecdotes, or mechanical long-short-long sentence alternation?

## Words to cut

1. Are banned words, filler phrases, often-empty adverbs, and inflated claims removed unless quoted as examples?

## Patterns to cut

1. Are binary contrasts, negative listings, rhetorical setups, and throat-clearing openers removed?
2. Are faux-insight setups, colon reveals, superficial analysis, fake-strong verbs, synonym cycling, dramatic fragments, and robotic rhythm fixed?
3. Are importance puffery and weasel attribution replaced with plain facts and named sources, or flagged for the user when no source exists?
4. Are fake-profound kicker lines deleted instead of rewritten into better metaphors?
5. Are summary-recap endings cut so the piece ends on a concrete point, takeaway, or next action?
6. Is formatting slop removed: Emoji headings, decorative bold, bullets that should be prose, headers over tiny sections?
7. Are colons sentence case unless grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code requires otherwise?
8. Are em dashes used sparingly: Usually none in short copy, and only 1-2 in longer drafts when they clearly help?

## Statistical fingerprints (perplexity, burstiness, cohesion, clustering, symmetry)

1. **Perplexity.** Are default, statistically-likely phrases replaced with sharper, simpler, slightly more specific word choices — without reaching for a thesaurus or inventing colorful language? Does the draft avoid runs of "performs an important role in / has a significant impact on / provides an opportunity to" style phrasing?
2. **Burstiness.** Does the draft mix sentence lengths naturally, with no paragraph where every sentence is roughly the same medium length? Are there occasional short punchy sentences and occasional longer winding sentences — based on the thought, not a mechanical alternation pattern?
3. **Cohesion.** Is friction restored where the thought has friction — i.e., are backtracks, qualifications, asides, and jumps kept or restored instead of sanded to a glass-smooth "Furthermore / Moreover / In addition" flow? Are explicit transition words cut when the relationship between sentences is already obvious? Do sentences sometimes start mid-stream ("Which is why…", "So…", "Wait —") rather than every sentence opening with a formal transition?
4. **Vocabulary clustering.** When two or more words from the same semantic family cluster in a short span (e.g., efficient/effective/optimize/streamline/productivity), have they been reduced to the one doing real work — with the rest replaced by plain language or the writer's own words, not cycled through synonyms?
5. **Paragraph symmetry.** Are paragraphs of genuinely varying length and shape — a one-sentence paragraph next to a longer one where that's what the content calls for? Have paragraphs that followed a rigid topic-elaboration-example-wrap shape been restructured to match how the ideas actually flow? Have two short paragraphs been merged where they're one thought, without chopping or padding to manufacture variety?
6. **Over-explained structure.** Are meta-signposts ("In this section we examine…", "As demonstrated above…") cut so the ideas stand on their own without a tour guide?

## Human voice markers

1. Are contractions kept (don't, can't, it's, I'm) unless the piece's formality forbids them — rather than being de-contracted into stiff "do not / cannot / it is" form?
2. Are first- and second-person (I, you, we) kept where natural, instead of being replaced with passive or impersonal constructions?
3. Are honest hedges kept when they reflect real uncertainty ("maybe," "I think," "as far as I can tell"), and cut only when they're empty filler?
4. Are genuine asides and parentheticals kept when they sound like the writer thinking, rather than edited out for neatness?
5. Are occasional deliberate sentence fragments kept when they land a point, without stacking fragments in dramatic AI-slop rhythm?
6. Are the writer's natural colloquialisms ("stuff," "thing," "ends up," "turns out") preserved instead of being elevated to formal generic language?
7. Are specific idiosyncratic details (times, places, anecdotes, admissions the user included) protected rather than smoothed away?
8. Did the edit preserve — not invent — personal voice markers? Are there zero manufactured "human touches" (fake typos, added "like" or "um," invented personal stories)?

## Final read

1. Was the edit checked directly against this file without requiring separate editor and evaluator agents?
2. Does the draft avoid robotic symmetry, repeated sentence shapes, and stacked punchy fragments?
3. Would the writer recognize the edited draft as their own voice?
4. Would the edited draft sound natural if read to a sharp colleague?
5. Does the final output include the full edited draft and a short **What changed** section?
6. For detect requests, does the response name each pattern with a quoted line and a short fix, without rewriting, scoring, or claiming AI authorship?
7. Does the draft read like a specific person talking to a specific reader — not a statistically average text generator, and not a caricature of a human either?
